1. About This Policy

I am the independent developer of Neon, an open-source Discord bot for running CTF challenges, handling submissions, recording winners, and maintaining leaderboards.

This policy explains the data Neon may process when it is used. If you run your own instance, you are responsible for how that instance is hosted, configured, and operated.

2. Information Neon May Process

Depending on the features used and the bot's configuration, Neon may process:

  • Discord user IDs and usernames needed to identify participants.
  • Discord server, channel, and role IDs needed to operate within a server.
  • Challenge titles, descriptions, point values, status, and related metadata.
  • Submission timestamps and Discord message IDs used to track submissions.
  • Winner records, awarded points, solve durations, and leaderboard information.
  • Administrative actions and audit timestamps.
  • A keyed HMAC digest representing an expected challenge flag, if enabled by the implementation.
Expected flags and submitted flags: Neon is designed not to store the original expected flags or submitted flags in its database. Expected flags are represented by a keyed HMAC-SHA256 digest, while submitted flags may appear in public Discord messages for manual review. The actual behavior depends on the version and configuration being operated; server administrators should verify the implementation before using it.

Discord may independently process information when users interact with Discord services.

3. Why Data Is Processed

The information above may be used to:

  • Identify participants and associate their submissions with the correct user.
  • Manage challenges and their lifecycle.
  • Record submissions, winners, points, and solve durations.
  • Display rankings and challenge history.
  • Maintain administrative audit trails and help investigate misuse.

Neon is not intended to sell personal data or serve advertisements.

4. Database and Hosting

Neon uses PostgreSQL to store the records required by its features.

I do not automatically have access to databases belonging to people who independently self-host Neon. I cannot describe the practices of an independent server operator; that operator is responsible for its own hosting provider, database permissions, logs, backups, and access controls.

If you use an instance operated by someone else, contact that operator to ask how your information is handled.

5. Public Discord Messages

If a server uses public flag submissions, the content of your submission may be visible to members who can access the relevant Discord channel.

Deleting a database record does not necessarily remove a Discord message, and deleting a Discord message does not necessarily remove a corresponding database record. The server operator must manage each system separately.

6. Data Retention and Deletion

Records may be retained for challenge history, leaderboard calculations, winner records, and audit purposes for as long as the instance operator considers necessary.

Retention and deletion practices may differ between independently operated instances. Contact the relevant server administrator or operator for requests concerning their instance.

7. Security

The bot is intended to use appropriate safeguards, but no software or hosting environment can be guaranteed completely secure.

Operators should protect bot tokens and secret keys, restrict database access, keep dependencies updated, and secure their hosting environments. Never share bot tokens or cryptographic secrets publicly.

8. Children's Privacy

Neon does not intentionally collect information beyond what is needed for its functionality. Server operators must ensure their use of Neon complies with applicable privacy laws and any age-related requirements that apply to their community.

9. Changes to This Policy

I may update this policy when the bot's features or data handling change. The latest version will be published at this site/URL.
